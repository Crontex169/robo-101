Kinematics Workshop

How does a robot arm know where its hand is, and which joint angles put it where you want? This course answers that from the ground up: vectors and rotations first, then forward and inverse kinematics and the Jacobian. Every idea comes with a live scene you can drag and test.

Format

How a lesson works

Each lesson takes 30–50 minutes and is about one idea. You read a short explanation first, then try it yourself in a live scene, and finish by checking what stuck.

Read first

The main text builds intuition with words and pictures. No idea is used before it has been explained.

Then try it

Labs put a live arm on the page. Each mission has a goal, a prediction to make first, and an explanation when you get there.

Under the hood

Formulas and derivations sit in fold-out blocks. Open them when you want the math behind what you just saw.

Check yourself

A short warm-up opens each lesson, and a quiz on common misconceptions closes it, with the reasoning for every answer.

Program

Program

Seven parts. Two short toolkits cover the math and physics you'll need, then five parts take you from what a robot arm is to steering one with its Jacobian. Each part ends with a checkpoint.

Terms from the lessons, with short definitions, are collected in the course glossary.

"Completed" and "started" marks are stored only in this browser.

Audience

Who is this for

Anyone curious about how robot arms move, starting from zero. If you remember a little school algebra and trigonometry, that's enough: everything else is taught along the way.

You'll learn to

  • describe where things are and how they're turned, with frames and rotation matrices
  • compute where an arm's hand is from its joint angles
  • find joint angles that put the hand where you want, and see when there are several answers or none
  • use the Jacobian to relate joint speeds to hand speeds and joint torques to hand forces

You don't need

  • a robot: every lab runs in the browser
  • prior robotics or linear algebra: the M and F parts cover what's needed
  • to install anything or sign up
FAQ

Questions

What do I need to know before starting?
Basic school algebra and the idea of sine and cosine. Part M (math toolkit) and Part F (physics toolkit) cover vectors, matrices, derivatives, rotation and torque before the main parts use them.
How long does it take?
A lesson takes 30–50 minutes. You can stop anywhere: your progress is remembered in this browser.
Is it free?
Yes. There's no sign-up and nothing to install. The site doesn't collect any data about you.
